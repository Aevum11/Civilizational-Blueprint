## 6. Non-Brittleness — Three Mechanisms

### 6.1 Compressive Reinforcement

F_vdW ∝ 1/a³. Thinner edge → stronger compression. The force gradient points INWARD (toward smaller gap). Any perturbation that increases the gap faces a NET restoring force from the surrounding intact edge. This is dynamic stability — the zero point IS a potential well with walls at 7.86 GPa/nm.

A chip opens the gap locally from 0.3 nm to, say, 1 nm. The compressive force drops from 786 MPa to 21 MPa at the chip site. But the surrounding intact edge still exerts 786 MPa, squeezing the chip shut. The pressure differential (765 MPa) acts as a crack-arrest stress far exceeding anything in conventional fracture mechanics.

### 6.2 Impedance Gradient Toughening

Along the taper, ξ(d) varies continuously between 1.0 (d=12) and 8.56 (d=1) with no sharp transitions. This means the structural reinforcement varies smoothly — no stress concentration points, no grain boundaries, no phase transitions. Crack nucleation requires a stress concentration site. The impedance gradient provides none.

### 6.3 Vacuum Self-Healing

When a chip removes material, new surfaces are exposed. These surfaces experience Casimir/vdW attraction toward the opposing face, tending to close the chip. The healing force is strongest for the smallest chips (F ∝ 1/a³). Partial healing occurs spontaneously — the vacuum pulls damaged regions back toward the equilibrium gap.
