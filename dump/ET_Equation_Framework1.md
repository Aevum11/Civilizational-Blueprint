## 3. Real-World Mathematics: What Equations Are {#3-real-world}

### 3.1 The Standard View

In standard mathematics, an equation is a statement that two expressions are equal: a = b. The equals sign (=), introduced by Robert Recorde in 1557, asserts that the left and right sides denote the same mathematical object. Equations are classified by structure: algebraic (polynomial), differential (involving derivatives), integral, functional, and Diophantine (integer solutions only). The act of "solving" an equation means finding the values of unknowns that make the equality hold.

### 3.2 The Philosophy of Equations

The deeper question — what does "equality" MEAN? — has occupied mathematicians and philosophers from Frege to Wittgenstein to modern category theory. Frege distinguished sense (the path to a referent) from reference (the object itself): "a = b" says two different senses (paths) reach the same reference (object). This is precisely the D-path convergence definition in ET. Category theory formalizes equality through isomorphism: objects are "equal" when there exist structure-preserving maps between them. Homotopy type theory goes further, treating equalities as paths in a space — again, convergence of distinct routes to a shared destination.

The most celebrated equations in physics and mathematics — E=mc², e^(iπ)+1=0, F=ma, a²+b²=c² — each contain numerical constants (c, e, π, φ, √2, α, h, G) that are not free parameters but structural features of reality.
