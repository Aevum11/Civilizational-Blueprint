## 3. Real-World Finance: What Markets Actually Are {#3-real-world}

### 3.1 The Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH)

The dominant academic theory of financial markets is the Efficient Market Hypothesis, developed by Eugene Fama in the 1960s. The EMH states that asset prices fully reflect all available information, making it impossible to consistently outperform the market through stock selection or market timing. The EMH exists in three forms:

- **Weak form:** Prices reflect all past trading data (historical prices and volume). Technical analysis cannot consistently generate excess returns.
- **Semi-strong form:** Prices reflect all publicly available information. Fundamental analysis cannot consistently generate excess returns.
- **Strong form:** Prices reflect ALL information, including insider knowledge. No one can consistently generate excess returns.

Decades of empirical research show mixed but broadly supportive results for weak and semi-strong forms: professional fund managers as a group do not consistently beat passive index strategies after accounting for fees.

The aggregate of millions of indeterminate agents is not a deterministic system with noise. It is an irreducibly indeterminate system that no D-set can fully capture.
