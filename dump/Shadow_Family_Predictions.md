## 2. Redistribution at N = 60: Combinatorics, Not Discovery

At N = 12, every known particle has d ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}. At N = 60, the divisor set expands to {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60}, and particles redistribute across all 12 families.

**Critical combinatorial fact:** At N = 60, simple families (d ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12}) occupy only 12 of 60 k-mod-60 positions (20%). Shadow families (d ∈ {5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 60}) occupy the remaining 48 positions (80%). The combinatorial expectation is therefore that ~80% of particles will land in shadow families at N = 60, regardless of any physics.

The actual figure: 178 of 227 (78.4%) land in shadow families — slightly BELOW the combinatorial expectation. This redistribution is an arithmetic consequence of increasing N, not a structural discovery. The shadow families at N = 60 are not "where BSM physics lives" — they are where 80% of the lattice positions are.

The structurally meaningful question is not "how many particles move to shadow families" but "what structural features of individual particles become visible at higher resolution?" The tower escalation reveals each particle's engagement with higher primes (5, 7, 11, ...), and the true-home resolution reveals structural depth. These are genuine structural properties. The raw redistribution percentage is not.

**Combinatorial context:** The d = 10 family at N = 60 contains 21 particles. Any two of those 21 particles "share d = 10" — that's C(21, 2) = 210 possible pairs. The muon-Higgs pairing is one of 210. The pion is also in d = 10 at N = 60, as are the Λ baryon, multiple χ_c states, and multiple bottomonium states. The d = 10 family is the fourth most populated shadow family.

**Structural check:** The muon has ε₆₀ = −9.755¢ (moderate — not near the lattice node). The Higgs has ε₆₀ = +2.979¢ (relatively low). They are separated by 612 k-units = 10.2 octaves. There is no additional structural evidence (matched ε, similar tower behavior, or resonance in the separation) that would elevate this pairing above combinatorial expectation.

**Physical context:** In the Standard Model, the Higgs gives the muon its mass through the muon Yukawa coupling. This is a real physical connection, but the Higgs gives ALL massive particles their masses. The d = 10 pairing does not distinguish the muon from 19 other particles that also share this family.

**Verdict:** Noted as an observation, not a discovery. Additional evidence would be needed to claim structural significance.

The honest claim is: **IF an undiscovered particle exists in the 6.5–9.0 GeV range, the Sempaevum lattice constrains its d = 5 mass to 7288 ± 211 MeV.** The lattice provides the WHERE. Experiment determines the WHETHER.
